# slo-alert

> Versão em português: [README.pt-BR.md](README.pt-BR.md)

How does a sentence like "99% of requests must succeed" become a page at the right moment, and stop when the problem stops? This mini-project takes a small shop service from the objective to the alert: indicators, error budget, burn rate, Prometheus rules tested with `promtool`, and an alert that fires when local k6 overloads the service and resolves after the load ends.

Code: MP-OBS-3. Full explanation: [docs/en/observability/slo-alert.md](../../../docs/en/observability/slo-alert.md).

```
k6 (local only) --overload--> shop --/metrics--> Prometheus --alerts--> Alertmanager --webhook--> receiver
                               ^                    |
        normal users ----------+        recording rules: SLI -> burn rate -> alert (two windows)
```

## The objectives

| Objective (over 30 days) | Indicator (SLI) | Error budget | Pages when the burn rate is above |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Availability: 99% of requests do not fail | failed (5xx) / all requests | 1% | 14.4, in the 5 m **and** the 1 m window |
| Latency: 95% of successful requests take 100 ms or less | 1 - bucket `le="0.1"` / count | 5% | 6, in the 5 m **and** the 1 m window |

Burn rate = bad share / error budget. At burn rate 1 the budget lasts exactly the 30 days. At 14.4 it is gone in 720 / 14.4 = 50 hours, and one hour at that pace spends 14.4 / 720 = 2% of it.

Production rules of this kind use windows of 1 hour and 5 minutes. The lab uses 5 minutes and 1 minute, twelve times shorter, so the demonstration takes three minutes. The thresholds and their arithmetic are the same.

## Results

Generated by `docker compose run --rm demo`, committed in [results/results.md](results/results.md). k6 ran 30 virtual users for 60 seconds against a shop that serves 8 requests at a time; about 5 requests per second of normal traffic ran all the time.

| Measured | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Peak request rate | 227 requests per second |
| Peak share of failed requests (1 m window) | 80.2% (objective: at most 1%) |
| Peak share of slow successful requests (1 m window) | 100% (objective: at most 5%) |
| Alert pending or firing before the load | no |

| Alert | Pending | Firing and receiver notified | Resolved after the load stopped | Peak burn rate | Burn rate of the 5 m window at resolution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `AvailabilityBudgetBurn` | 4 s after the load started | 14 s | 53 s | 80.2 | 77.7 |
| `LatencyBudgetBurn` | 6 s | 16 s | 51 s | 20.0 | 17.3 |

**Documented times, asserted by the end-to-end test:** the receiver is notified **within 60 seconds** of the load starting, and the alert resolves **within 120 seconds** of the load stopping.

The last column is the lesson of the two windows. When the alerts resolved, the 5-minute window was still burning far above the threshold (77.7 against 14.4): it still remembered the incident. The 1-minute window had already forgotten, and the rule needs both. With the long window alone the page would have stayed on for about five minutes after the service was healthy again.

## Quiz topics it demonstrates

- `observability` / `sli-slo-error-budgets`: SLI as good or bad events over valid events, the objective, the error budget, a latency SLI as a share of requests under a threshold and not an average, burn rate
- `observability` / `alerting`: symptom-based alerts on budget burn, two windows, `for` with pending and firing, Alertmanager grouping and resolved notifications
- `observability` / `metric-types-cardinality`: a counter and a classic histogram written by hand, cumulative `le` buckets, `rate()`, why a series must start at 0
- `observability` / `prometheus-grafana-loki-tempo`: Prometheus pulling `/metrics`, PromQL in recording rules

## Run

The only requirement is Docker.

```sh
./setup-unix-slo-alert.sh        # Linux and macOS
./setup-windows-slo-alert.ps1    # Windows
```

The script builds the image and runs, in order: the unit tests, the Prometheus rule tester, the proof that k6 refuses a non-local target, and the end-to-end incident (about three minutes). Everything is removed at the end.

## Demo

```sh
docker compose run --rm demo
docker compose down -v
```

Starts the stack and k6 (45 quiet seconds, 60 seconds of load), prints the timeline as it happens and rewrites `results/results.md` and `results/timeline.json`.

### The rules and their tests

[`prometheus/rules.yaml`](prometheus/rules.yaml) has 9 recording rules and 2 alerting rules, in three layers:

| Layer | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Indicator | `job:slo_errors_per_request:ratio_rate5m` | Share of failed requests in the last 5 minutes |
| Burn rate | `job:slo_availability_burn_rate:ratio_rate5m` | That share divided by the 1% budget |
| Budget | `job:slo_availability_error_budget_remaining:ratio_1h` | 1 = untouched, 0 = spent, negative = overspent |
| Alert | `AvailabilityBudgetBurn` | Both burn rates above 14.4, held for 10 s |

[`prometheus/rules.test.yaml`](prometheus/rules.test.yaml) is run by `promtool test rules` inside the pinned Prometheus image. It simulates four histories in milliseconds: a healthy service, a constant 10% of failures (burn rate 10: objective broken, but below the paging threshold), an outage of ten minutes (fires, then resolves 90 seconds after the end while the 5-minute window still burns at 23.3), and a service that is slow but never fails (pages on latency only).

## Local only

- k6 (`grafana/k6:2.3.0`) reads its target from `TARGET`, which defaults to a local address, and [`load/target.js`](load/target.js) refuses any host outside a closed list (`localhost`, `127.0.0.1`, `[::1]`, `shop`). `load-refusal-test` proves it, and the unit tests cover look-alikes such as `http://localhost@example.com`.
- The compose network is `internal` and no port is published. To look at Prometheus by hand, use `docker compose exec prometheus wget -qO- 'http://127.0.0.1:9090/api/v1/alerts'`.
- The "pager" is a local webhook that only remembers what it received. k6 usage reporting is disabled; Prometheus and Alertmanager send none.

## Structure

| Path | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `ts/src/metrics.ts` | A counter and a histogram by hand, in the Prometheus text format |
| `ts/src/shop.ts` | The service: fixed capacity, slower with each request in flight, 503 when full |
| `ts/src/slo.ts` | The arithmetic: error budget, burn rate, time to exhaustion, the two-window condition |
| `ts/src/receiver.ts` | The webhook that receives Alertmanager notifications |
| `ts/src/watch.ts`, `ts/src/demo.ts` | The watcher that records the timeline of an incident, and the demo |
| `prometheus/` | Scrape configuration, the rules and their `promtool` tests |
| `alertmanager/` | Routing, grouping and the webhook receiver |
| `load/` | The k6 script, the local-target rule and its refusal test |
| `results/` | The committed timeline |

No metrics client library is used; the exposition format is written by hand because that is part of the lesson.

## Notes

- The scrape and evaluation intervals are 2 seconds and Alertmanager waits 1 second before the first notification. Production values are 15 to 60 seconds and 30 seconds. They only make the lab fast; they are not a recommendation.
- The error budget rule looks at the last hour because the lab has only minutes of data. A real one looks at the whole window of the objective (`[30d]`).
- The times in the results depend on the machine, but only by a few seconds: they are dominated by the windows (`for: 10s`, and the 1-minute window that must empty before the alert resolves).
